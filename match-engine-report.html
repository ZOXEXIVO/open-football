How the Match Engine
Plays a Game of Football

There is no single engine.rs — the match engine is a 421-file module tree under src/core/src/match/engine/, centred on a stateless type called FootballEngine<W, H> defined in engine/engine/mod.rs. It simulates a full match as millions of 10 ms ticks: the ball flies with real ballistics, 22 players think through state machines, a referee officiates, coaches adapt, and nerves rise and fall — until the final whistle produces a full result, ratings and highlights.

A match in miniature — the loop that turns 10 ms ticks into 90 minutes of football
core loop

⚽Tick-driven

Each tick is 10 ms of match time. A half is 45 minutes ⇒ ~270 000 ticks. Full and “light” ticks alternate for performance.

stateless

🧭Stateless engine

FootballEngine carries no fields. All state lives in MatchField, MatchContext and the per-tick GameTickContext.

layered

🪜Layered AI

Ball physics → contests → player state machines → team tactics → coach → psychology. Each layer only reads the layers below it.

1
Entry

Entry & configuration — before a ball is kicked

Three entry points, one config struct. The engine builds the whole world of the match from two squads and a MatchEngineConfig.

Entry pointWhat it does
play(left_squad, right_squad, recordings, friendly, knockout)Classic entry — builds a default config from two flags.
play_seeded(…, seed: Option<u64>)Pins the engine's owned RNG stream. Every calibrated roll — substitution timing, penalty shootout, foul cards, corner contests, player decisions — becomes reproducible. None = OS entropy.
play_with_config(squads, MatchEngineConfig)Full control: seed, fixture date, weather/pitch/crowd/importance/derby, referee profile, friendly/knockout flags, recording switch.

What play_with_config builds, in order

Score & starting tactics

A fresh Score, and a snapshot of both teams' kickoff tactic type (home is always on the left at kickoff).

MatchStandard::measure

Measures the standard of football this fixture will be played at — used downstream for result quality checks and blowout detection.

Players & field

MatchPlayerCollection::from_squads and MatchField::new(W, H, …) — the pitch, both squads, and the recording buffer.

MatchContext::new_with_config

The world: clock, score, environment, referee, seeded RNG, coach handles, plans. Psychology is seeded per player, and the chemistry map is built from the kickoff XI — pair scores feed the pass evaluator's one-touch bonus from the first whistle.

Home-crowd arousal

Crowd intensity × home advantage is stamped onto every player (starters and bench) as a multiplicative effective-skill edge — tuned across multiple 400-match titrations to land the real ~45/25/30 home/away/draw split.

Kickoff

StateManager::kick_off_period puts the ball at the centre spot with the home side on it — no 14-second dead patch waiting for the emergency chaser override.

⚙ Seeding is load-bearing: one RNG stream is shared by every calibrated decision in the match. A change that reorders draws — even in a branch that never fires for benchless squads — reshuffles every roll downstream. That is why calibration harnesses pin seeds.
2
Outer loop

The match lifecycle — a 9-state machine

StateManager walks the match through MatchState, calling play_inner for every timed state and a dedicated run_penalty_shootout when a knockout tie demands it.

Timed states run the tick loop · gold = intervals (no ticks, free substitutions, sides swap) · the shootout is a discrete kick model

⏱Timed states

Run play_inner — the tick loop. The clock only runs here.

🔄Intervals

Half-time and extra-time interval: no ticks, no play. The single most common substitution minute in real football — a change here is free (no window spent), so open_interval opens a sub window that plays out at the first stoppage of the next half.

🥅Shootout

When the states are exhausted and the tie is not: a discrete penalty-kick model with its own conversion probabilities and save credits.

3
Inner loop

The heartbeat — play_inner, one tick at a time

This is the loop that actually plays football. It runs until increment_time() says the period is over — and, like the laws of the game, it refuses to end while a penalty is in flight.

Every pass through the body is one simulated 10 ms — the two “cadence” cards fire far less often than the tick itself

The cadences (one loop body, several clocks)

ConcernCadenceWhy
Match clock+10 ms / tickBase granularity — ballistics need it.
Full AI vs light tickalternatingHalf the ticks skip player thinking; movement continues on stored velocities. Saves ~half the AI cost.
Position recordingevery 30 msReplay samples, on a cursor so half-restarts keep the exact cadence.
Coach evaluationevery 500 ticks ≈ 5 sThe touchline reacts in seconds, not milliseconds. Also probes situational formation overrides (chasing / protecting shapes).
Tactical refresh25 ticks base · 10 in transitionsAdaptive: a 40-tick window after any transition trigger (possession swing, goal, coach change, attacking-third entry/exit) uses the fast cadence so phases resolve crisply.
Medical roll3–8′, then 6–14′Injury risk drawn from the shared RNG; replacements wait for dead balls, checked once per second.
Substitution plan10–20′, re-arm 5–15′“Something around the hour” — but a pressure look can act between reviews when the game has gone wrong.
Dead-ball window45–75 s after a goal · 12 s for a subCutscene ticks: no ball physics, no AI, no events — the celebration choreography runs instead.
⏳ The clock outlasts the period: while increment_time() || ball.penalty_in_progress(…) — the laws extend the half for a penalty still to be taken or travelling. The loop only ends when both agree.
4
The work

A full tick, phase by phase — game_tick_inner

The full tick is the engine's entire brain in one pass. Eight profiled phases, run in a strict order. Hover the pipeline to read each phase; the order is not negotiable — several phases exist to fix ordering bugs.

phase 0 · tick context
P_TICKCTX

tick_ctx.update — freeze the world

The per-tick snapshot every decision reads: spatial grid, space buffers, the id→position roster join, the per-side first-to-the-ball chase table. Built once; all 22 players read the same frozen world — bit-identical and cache-friendly.

phase 1 · ball
P_BALL

Aerial contest resolves before the ball is claimable

resolve_cross_contest runs first: a lofted delivery descending into the claim ceiling (2.8 m) is attacked in the air before any receiver claim can bring it down — this ordering recovered the whole aerial game (~40 lofted deliveries a match).

P_BALL

play_ball — Ball::update (Step 6)

Physics, contests, boundaries, restarts, ownership. Then: pending set-piece teleport → save credit → foul-advantage resolution → tick_ctx.refresh_ball so the player phase sees the current owner (prevents the TakeBall force-override firing for a player who already has the ball).

phase 2 · players
P_PLAYERS

play_players — 22 minds, one at a time

Each player's state machine runs process: decide a transition, emit an event, contribute a velocity. Then movement integrates and the boundary clamps. Exertion, fatigue and injury risk accrue here.

phase 3 · events
P_DISPATCH

EventDispatcher::dispatch — drain everything

Every staged event fires: fouls, goals, restarts, stats. Then handle_goal_reset and — critically — a second apply_pending_set_piece_teleport: free kicks, penalties and offsides are awarded by event handlers after the first drain, and without the post-dispatch teleport they sat staged until the next tick, when move_to nulled the taker 30–200u from the spot (47 owner-drops a match before the fix).

Why this order exists — three lessons baked into the driver

🌤Aerial before claim

Claims resolved inside the receiver's priority pass would disarm the aerial contest that exists to stop exactly that — 22.9 disarmed contests per match. Running the contest first settles the race by construction.

🥅Drain after dispatch too

Set-piece teleports staged by event handlers must be applied in the same tick, or the next tick's move_to drops ownership — the ball dead, ownerless, “OWNER_TOO_FAR”.

⚽Refresh the ball view

Ownership can change inside play_ball (new claim, pass target receive). Without refresh_ball, player dispatch sees a stale owner.

📊 Measured cost (per the engine's own PhaseProf): a full tick spends its time on tick-context, ball, players and dispatch — with player AI alone roughly velocity 36% / process 32% / fatigue 11% / move 8% / loose-ball override 6%. The profiler is env-gated: OF_PHASE_PROF=1.
5
Performance

The light tick — physics without the thinking

Half of all ticks are game_tick_light: the ball integrates fully, players move on their stored velocities but do not re-think. One exception: a shot in flight wakes the goalkeepers.

Light ticks are full football minus the thinking — and the thinking is half the cost
  • State timers stay frozen — in_state_time counts AI ticks only. Advancing it on light ticks would halve every state timeout and double the fatigue curve's rate.
  • The keeper is never moved twice — during a shot, the GK branch runs first; the movement loop then skips both goalkeepers so a leap is not integrated twice (gravity twice, arc collapsed).
  • Sent-off players are left at their sentinel — a boundary clamp would drag them from (−500,−500) to the pitch corner and record a ghost replay sample.
  • The restart taker may leave the pitch — the one player allowed off it, to fetch a ball that went out.
6
The ball

Ball pipeline — Ball::update, 20+ stages a tick

One call, twenty decisions, in this exact order. The ball stages intents (a restart, a save credit); the engine finishes them a phase later — the only place that holds &mut MatchField.

entry gates

In the net?

The netting owns the ball; play is dead. The celebration drives it from here — nothing below applies (no pass to intercept, no shot to save).

Awaiting a restart?

Out of play: tick_awaited_restart integrates the ball until the hoardings stop it, then pins it. Everything below would fight the restart — chase signals would send an opponent to fetch it.

flight

Velocity integration

Gravity, air drag, spin (Magnus), roll friction — the ballistics step (details in Step 7).

Aerial delivery tick

A lofted pass/cross updates its delivery state, apex and landing predictions.

contests — anyone may touch it

Try intercept

Can a player cut the pass out before it arrives? Skill-based reach test.

Try aerial duel

Headers: two men jump; the stronger reach wins the knock-down.

Try block shot / try block pass

Bodies and legs in the lane — deflection, partial blocks, full stops.

Try save shot

The goalkeeper: reach, dive, split-step — or a goal if he cannot.

safety nets — the stall police

Notify standing ball

Signal states that the ball is there to be played.

Force claim if deadlocked

The nuclear option: ball unowned and stopped too long → someone takes it.

Force TakeBall if unowned too long

Unconditional safety net — nearest players are dispatched to the loose ball.

Position stall detection

The strict signal: ball has not moved anywhere in 1000 ticks — a real stall. (The old “owned stuck” detector fired on legitimate 6–12 s defender holds.)

ownership

Process ownership

Control claims, keeper pickups, pass-target receptions, the tracking distance that keeps the ball glued to a carrier.

Carry tracker

Dribble accounting — possession spells and the shots that end them.

Keeper body block

The keeper's own body as the last object that can touch the ball before it steps through him.

movement, then the law

move_to

Integrate the ball's step — its own velocity, or the owner-tracking step while carried.

Frame rebound → goal → over bar → wide → throw-in → boundary

In this order: the woodwork first (a frame hit has not crossed the line, gone over or out), then the goal line, then the other lines.

Police offside

The offside snapshot and line — who was beyond the second-last defender when the pass left the foot.

Update landing cache

Predictive landing point, cached for every AI consumer to read cheaply.

7
Ballistics

Ball physics & contests — real numbers

The ball is a genuine physics object: 3-D position with metric height, a spin vector, gravity and drag per tick. Strikes solve their spin from the same Magnus coefficient the integrator uses — the two can never drift apart.

🌀Flight model

GRAVITY_PER_TICK · AIR_DRAG_PER_TICK · GROUND_FRICTION for rolls · SpinModel: angular velocity set at strike time, integrated as Magnus force airborne, scrubbed on contact. The only channel that can turn a flight sideways.

🥊Contests

Interception, block, save, aerial duel, pass-block, tackle, reach, ownership, possession — each a skill-vs-skill roll gated by geometry (distance, flight height, claim ceiling 2.8 m).

🧱Boundary & restarts

The goal frame (posts the replay draws are the same geometry the physics rebounds off), the net, the run-off rectangle the restart taker may walk in, and the awaited-restart state machine: throw-in, goal kick, corner, foul walk.

ConstantValueMeaning
CONTROL_DISTANCE12 u = 1.5 mOne stride — a real first-touch distance. Must stay ≤ the owner-tracking cap (they once disagreed by 6× and 86% of shots were struck off loose balls).
LOOSE_CLAIM_DISTANCE5 uA fifty-fifty is won by the man who gets his foot on it; chasers settle to ball speed inside this radius.
MAX_OWNER_TRACK_DISTANCE / KICKABLE_DISTANCE15 u = 1.9 mHard cap on owner tracking; also the kick gate that ended 1150-passes-a-team (players were kicking balls 40 m away, destroying passes in flight).
ASSIST_WINDOW_TICKS600 ≈ 6 sAssist eligibility backstop — long ball flight + touch + strike, excluding the goal-kick “assist” for a solo run half a minute later.
Claim ceiling (aerial)2.8 mHeight below which a lofted delivery enters the contest band and must be won in the air.
⚖️ Units are split on purpose: players move in 0.125 m grid units on the horizontal plane, while vertical speed and the ball's z are metric. The two axes are kept apart so a leap can't be scaled by a limiter expressed in the wrong unit — the bug that once left goalkeepers computing textbook sin arcs and never leaving the ground.

What each state machine decides with

📐Positions & closure

GameTickContext hands every state the frozen snapshot: a spatial grid, distance closures, ball metadata, waypoint targets. Aggregates are memoised per (player, tick) — bit-identical, computed once.

🧮Skill composites & ops

Attacking ops: shooting with a single xG model (ShotSkillProfile::expected_xg — distance, angle, keeper, pressure, shot type multipliers), passing evaluator (one-touch chemistry bonus, through balls, crosses, flanks), support movement. Defensive ops: pressing, marking, panic clearance, goal-side recovery. Duel ops: dribble duel, marker evasion.

🧬Body, fatigue & traits

Condition processor burns condition by ActivityIntensity × MovementEffort; injury rolls ride the same pass; traits (PPMs) bias decisions; tackle_cooldown stops 40-foul first-five-minutes; decision-commitment guards stop A→B→A flickering.

The team

Teamplay & tactics — the game above the players

Individual states branch on a shared reading of the game. TeamTacticalState classifies each side's situation into an 8-phase model, refreshed every 10–25 ticks; the MatchCoach sits above it, moving an instruction ladder every 5 seconds.

The 8 game phases — a team reads the game, then every state machine follows the reading

🎛️The coach ladder

MatchCoach::evaluate reads score, clock, fatigue and rolling metrics (xG for/against, shots, pressures, deep entries, dangerous turnovers), then picks an instruction. Each instruction is a table of decision biases consumed by the passing / shooting / movement scorers.

WasteTime · 0.0 ParkTheBus · 0.1 SlowDown · 0.3 Normal · 0.5 PushForward · 0.75 AllOutAttack · 1.0

One continuous ambition axis — no if is_relegated special cases; the same ladder covers every situation.

🧪Chemistry, shape & standard

Kickoff-latched pair chemistry feeds the one-touch passing bonus. Team plans (attack / defence / shape / wide) position the unit. MatchStandard::measure rates the standard of football for downstream quality gates. Leadership: the loudest organising voice (keeper included) lifts the whole back line.

10
The referee

Officiating — the laws, as a system

The referee is not a coin-flip bolted onto tackles. It is a full subsystem: profiles, advantage, offside snapshots, restart shapes, set-piece routines, and game management.

🧑‍⚖️Referee profile

Strictness and a marginal-call bias are configured per fixture — the officiating half of home advantage, stacked on top of the crowd-arousal half. The same foul reads differently under a strict referee.

⚖️Fouls & advantage

FoulResolver supports deferred fouls with an advantage window, ticked every full tick — cheap in the dominant no-advantage case. Professional fouls, dissent and time-wasting are managed as game states, not scoreboard hacks.

🚩Offside police

A snapshot of the line is taken when the pass leaves the foot — who was beyond the second-last defender at that instant — and checked at the receiver, so a late run is never punished and an early one never escapes.

📐Restart shapes

Kickoff plans with stations, corner shapes with arming and a sweep lifecycle, goal-kick run-ups, throw-ins, penalty stances. Every restart is a choreographed shape, not a teleport with a whistle.

🎯Set-piece routines

Corner routines are scored and picked per match with a repeat-penalty above an xG threshold; free-kick choices (shot / cross / pass) scored per band; penalty takers and saves scored. History-aware, so a side does not keep replaying a routine the defence has read.

⏸️Stoppages

Injury stoppages, the restart wait, and the added-time tally — context.tally.note_added_time records exactly how much stoppage time each period accrued.

11
The mind

Psychology — nerves, momentum, and skill under pressure

Every player carries a PsychState through the match. Events write to it; an aggregate pass reads it back into skill modifiers that feed effective skill — so a nervous debutant is genuinely a worse footballer until he plays himself in.

PsychologyState lives on MatchContext — seeded per player at kickoff, refreshed every tick

🌡️Two kinds of nerves

Kickoff nerves settle over minutes scaled by composure; event nerves (a booking) are permanent. Felt pressure at the closing stage of a close match is added on top.

📈Momentum

Short-lived boosts after goals/errors/red cards, per player and per team (TeamMomentum), with leadership damping the swings.

💥Action lapses

ActionLapse families — a keeper who has just fumbled is more likely to fumble the next one; the lapse model makes errors cluster like they do in real football.

Communication

Events & the dispatcher — how everything hears everything

Players and the ball never touch each other's internals. They emit events; the dispatcher applies them. Fouls, goals, restarts and stats all travel through one bus.

PieceDesignWhy it matters
EventBallEvent | PlayerEventTwo emitters, one bus.
EventCollectionInline slot for 1 event + boxed overflow VecThe hot path moves ~6M updates a match; 0–1 events is the dominant case. A 4-slot inline array alone was ~224 bytes of per-update move traffic.
EventDispatcherDrain + recursion buffer for chained follow-upsA goal resets the world and fans out new events; handlers can emit while dispatching.

🔌 Module boundaries are enforced by the bus: the ball stages an intent (“a restart is pending”), the engine — the only place holding &mut MatchField — finishes it a phase later. Nothing reaches sideways into another module.
13
The bench

Substitutions & injuries — the manager's clock

Three layers answer three questions: when the board goes up (urgency), who crosses the line (scoring), and the swap itself (the pass). Deciding and acting stay two steps — a change never stops the match; it waits for the ball to be dead.

⏰Urgency — when

One continuous pressure per side: the scoreline, the legs, the trouble on the pitch, and the manager's own temperament. A quiet game changes on the plan (“around the hour”); a game that has gone wrong changes when it goes wrong.

🎖️Scoring — who

Sub-off / sub-in fit, star protection (a key man on a booking), tactical need — ranked, capped per pass.

🏥Medical — forced

The in-match injury roll runs in any playing period: first check 3–8 minutes in, then every 6–14. Replacements wait for dead balls like every other change; the physio does not come on while the game is going.

🎬 The 12-second cutscene: a change plays out as a SubstitutionBreak — men walking on and off along the touchline, the board up, the clock stopped. Like the goal celebration, it is deliberately outside the state machine and outside the RNG stream, so it can never disturb calibrated rolls.

Full time

Result & ratings — what the whistle produces

After the last tick, build_result assembles everything the league pipeline consumes — and the rating pass turns raw stat lines into honest 1.0–10.0 marks.

Three moving parts and nothing else: performance value → scale → shape

📦MatchResultRaw

Score, added time, tally, weather/pitch, penalty shootout record, standard of football, per-team final squads and tactics — mapped back to home/away by team id, not field position.

🎞️HighlightSelector

The near-misses worth keeping and which provisional chance clips to hold — one call decides both, so a marker on the timeline never points into a grey zone.

💥Blowout guard

A result the squads say is impossible is dumped under match-logs for inspection — the engine's own tripwire against calibration regressions.

Observability

Diagnostics — the engine watches itself

The engine ships with a permanent debug arsenal, most of it behind the match-logs feature and env flags — not because anything is broken, but because a football simulator's regressions are invisible in a final score.

⏱️PhaseProf

Env-gated per-phase timing (OF_PHASE_PROF=1): tick-context, ball, players, dispatch, tactical, coach, light. Thread-local accumulators sum per worker under rayon.

🧪Censuses

Heatmaps, defensive-shape spacing, box episodes, possession geography, duel gates, loose-chase samples — the numbers that prove the football looks like football.

🔎Teleport probe

A whole-tick relocation census: every checkpoint between phases closes the window on the function above it — any ball movement it books was a write, not physics.

🕸️Transition graph

Audits the state machines' reachability: every transition recorded, dead ends and invariant violations reported — a player state machine that cannot be exited is a bug the scoreboard would never show.

🎯Calibration harness

Seeded 400-fixture sweeps, A/B controls like home_flat, and per-arm goal-gap measurement — the titrations that keep every constant honest.

🩺Frame traces

On-demand ball/keeper traces around specific moments (a skied ball crossing a height, a save, a restart) — the frame-level evidence behind every ordering fix in this report.
